# Quantum Forge: Blade Evolution

A Roblox incremental where clicking a forge builds heat, holding the heat in the
sweet spot multiplies your yield, and the blade you forge changes the weapon on your
character. Prestige buys new rules, not bigger numbers.

**Everything is code.** No models, no marketplace assets, no Studio-authored UI. The
whole game — geometry, interface, icons — is generated from `src/` and `scripts/`.
Clone it, `rojo serve`, press Play.

> [!warning] Superseded in places
> This is the long-form project record through Forge 12. Forge 13 replaced the tab
> strip and side panel with the Foundry Instrument rail, deleted the forge roll
> column, and replaced the heat lockout with the 90% lean point. Where this note
> describes the old UI or the old heat rule, the ADR log wins: ADR-076…082. The
> mechanics that have not changed are still accurate.

## Quick start

Requires [Rojo](https://rojo.space) 7.x (and [Rokit](https://github.com/rojo-rbx/rokit)
if you want to pin it).

```powershell
rojo plugin install     # once, installs the Studio plugin
rojo serve              # start the live sync server
```

In Studio: **Rojo → Connect**, address `localhost:34872`, then press **Play**.

The server prints:

```
[QuantumForge] remotes created
[QuantumForge] monetization connected
[QuantumForge] autosave started
[QuantumForge] actions registered
[QuantumForge] lobby constructed
```

and the client prints a `[QuantumForge] ui built in …ms (content …x…)` line.

No API key, no upload, and no asset import is needed — `AssetManifest` falls back to
`rbxassetid://0` and the UI draws vector glyphs.

### One-shot build

```powershell
rojo build -o verify.rbxlx
```

Open the `.rbxlx` directly in Studio to test without the plugin, or to publish.

## Systems

### The forge loop

Clicking adds 2.5 heat; heat decays at 5/s. Sustained clicking above **2 clicks per
second** makes heat climb; below that it falls. Holding 65–85 pays 2.5x. Above 85 you
can vent for Aether; past 90 every strike pays half until you purge. Heat caps at 100
and the anvil never locks (ADR-079).

The full derivation is in [`game-design-math.md`](game-design-math.md).

Three layers sit on top of the heat loop:

- **Streak.** Ten in-band strikes in a row open a 15 second window where every
  strike pays double. A miss or a burn-out breaks it. The ten pips under the anvil
  show how close the window is, and the line becomes `OVERDRIVE x2 12s` while it
  is open.
- **Anomalies.** At 55% heat or above, a strike has a 6% chance to drop two Void
  Shards; during Overdrive the chance is 18%. Shards buy runes.
- **First minute.** A fresh save pays five times the normal yield until lifetime
  Star-Matter reaches 60, so the first click lands with some weight instead of a
  `+1`.

### Currencies

| Currency | Earned by | Spent on | Resets on ascend |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Star-Matter** | Clicking, drones | Blades | Yes |
| **Aether Shards** | Venting heat, dailies, playtime | Drones, expeditions | Yes |
| **Quantum Cores** | Ascending, expeditions | Tech tree | **Never** |
| **Void Shards** | Hot-gauge anomalies | Runes | **Never** |

### Progression

- **25 blade patterns** across 5 rarities, forged from five weighted banners. A
  roll picks the banner from lifetime Star-Matter, the rarity from its pool, and a
  variant (Standard, Tempered, Overclocked, Void-Touched, Prismatic) from its own
  table. The same blade can come out five ways, and every roll is kept as a copy.
  Duplicates melt into Scrap, which pays for variant rerolls. A pity counter forces
  a Void or Quantum on schedule, so a bad streak cannot deadlock the banner.
  The equipped copy renders in the preview frame with its variant tint on the
  emissive surfaces, and its rarity colours the aura on your character.
- **Five drone rungs**, no cap, producing passive Star-Matter scaled by the equipped
  blade. Scrap Drone (5 AE, 0.6/s) unlocks at 0 owned, Molten Core (150 AE, 22/s) at
  15, Quantum Vector (6 000 AE, 900/s) at 45, Celestial Swarm (250 000 AE, 42 000/s)
  at 120, Cosmic Dreadnought (12M AE, 2.4M/s) at 300. The gate is fleet size, not
  currency, and every rung pays better per Aether than the ceiling of the one below.
  The Fleet tab opens on a 3D stage: the selected rung turns in a `ViewportFrame`
  under a name plate, with the five rung keys and a BUILD 1 / BUILD 10 dock overlaid
  on the render. Rung 1 uses the one uploaded drone mesh; rungs 2-5 are procedural,
  so the whole ladder renders with no uploads. The same tab runs expeditions:
  committed drones leave the passive pool until the mission lands.
- **8 tech nodes** bought with Quantum Cores, including a wider sweet spot,
  auto-clicks, crits, and free starting blades.
- **Blade mastery** is per blade and permanent: ranks at 100, 400, 1 200, 3 000
  and 8 000 strikes unlock up to three rune sockets. Runes cost Void Shards and
  move strike yield, cooling, crit chance or vent payout. The Runes tab shows the
  whole chain in one column: rank bar, socket rack, rune shelf.
- **Expeditions** spend drones instead of counting them, taken off the cheapest rung
  first. Debris Sweep (5 drones, 5 min), Deep Salvage (15, 15 min) and Void Run
  (30, 60 min) pay Aether plus Cores and Shards at the deep end. The squad earns
  nothing while it is away, and the payout is locked at dispatch.
- **Ascension** at 1e9 run Star-Matter grants `floor((run/1e9)^0.5)` Cores.

### Network model

State is batched into one `StateSync` payload per player at 10 Hz. Discrete moments
(a crit, a vent, a forge, an ascension) use a separate `Event` remote so they are not
held back by the sync tick. Clicking is predicted locally against a pending-credit
buffer and reconciled on the next sync, so the number responds in one frame without
ever drifting from the server.

Action handlers receive `(player, payload)` — the verb was already consumed by the
dispatcher to pick the handler. Passing it through as a third argument meant every
handler read the action name as its payload: `forge` looked up a blade called
"forge", found nothing, and rejected with `Unknown blade`. Forging *and* equipping
were dead, and the onboarding step that asks for a blade could never complete. That
is the kind of bug that looks like a UI problem, because the only visible symptom is
a stack of red toasts.

### The anvil's feedback stack

Every accepted strike fires, in one frame: a floating `+yield` (with a second `x2.5`
marker in the band), a rarity-coloured slash flipbook, a ballistic spark burst, a
scale-punch on the blade container, a white/gold wash over the stage, a 3D slam from
the blade itself, a shaker on the button, and a pitched click. Crits add a bolt of
procedural lightning and a shallow flash. Forging adds a variant-ranked celebration
(punch, coloured bloom, panel shake, result stinger scored by variant) and showcases
the new blade for 2.6s even when it is not the one that got equipped. The stage
carries a pool of twelve ambient embers, recycled rather than spawned per frame.

The sound kit is 16 pooled slots over three proven `rbxasset://` samples. Every
button, tab, modal, roll, melt, reroll, cache, dispatch and ascension has a cue;
the strike chime climbs with the streak so the ear tracks the rail before the eye
does. The side-panel header holds a backpack and a gear; sound, screen effects and
shake are three switches in the settings sheet, persisted to the profile. Each
setting is a full-width row and the row is the switch: title, note and an `ON` /
`OFF` chip, with the whole 402px surface flipping it. The old
`SFX` text key is gone, because a mute that does not say what it mutes is a
puzzle.

Two rules keep it from becoming noise:

- **One animation entry point.** Everything routes through `UiKit.tween`, which
  collapses to zero duration under reduce-motion. Sparks and shake are skipped
  entirely rather than toned down.
- **Feedback never lies about the economy.** The `STRIKE SPEED` chip is a real
  clicks-per-second readout, not a hidden multiplier. Heat is shown by the glow's
  width and colour, which is exactly what the gauge says.

The blade is framed from its own measured bounds at a 77% fill, on a three-quarter
hero camera (26° azimuth, 11° elevation) with a continuous roll and a 2.3%-of-height
bob. Measured through a full turntable cycle on the live build: 70.8–77.1% of frame
height, never touching an edge, worst case included.

### The forge rolls; the inventory keeps

There is no longer a ladder. The forge card opens on the banner: the current tier,
its price, the pity meter and a single `ROLL` key. A roll resolves in this order:

1. **Banner.** Lifetime Star-Matter picks one of five tiers (Scrap, Molten, Astral,
   Void, Cosmic). Banner unlock is monotonic, so it cannot fall behind a dry streak.
2. **Rarity.** Weights are 55 / 28 / 12 / 4 / 1 across Scrap, Forged, Astral, Void
   and Quantum. A pity hit forces the Void-or-Quantum pool and resets the counter.
3. **Blade.** The tier pool holds one pattern per rarity, so a rarity roll is also
   a pattern roll.
4. **Variant.** Standard 74, Tempered 14, Overclocked 8, Void-Touched 3, Prismatic 1.
   The variant multiplies the blade's multiplier and can add crit, vent, cooling or
   Void Shard bonuses.

Every roll creates a copy with a uid. The vault groups identical (pattern,
variant) pairs into one card and prints `xN`, but equip, reroll and melt all act
on one copy; the uid is the identity a trade will move later. Melting pays Scrap
scaled by tier and variant rank, and Scrap is the only cost of a variant reroll.
`MELT MULTIPLE` arms a batch mode, `SELECT DUPES` picks every spare in one pass,
and a red `MELT n` plate at the right end of the bar settles the batch through a
single `salvageMany` behind a confirm. The last copy cannot be melted and the
equipped copy is never in a pick list, so a player cannot accidentally end up with
nothing to swing. The card badge prints `2/3` when one of three copies is protected,
and the bar counts copies rather than cards, so the number on the key is the number
the server will take.

### Prices scale with the account

A fixed price table stops being a sink the moment income outgrows it, and drones
multiply the passive yield by the blade multiplier. Every Star-Matter price
therefore takes a progress multiplier anchored on lifetime Star-Matter:

```
progressScale(lifetime) = min((lifetime / 1e9) ^ 0.5, 1e4)
```

It is exactly `1` below `1e9`, so no early-game price moved. The forge applies it
last, after its own `1.05` per-roll ramp (capped at `12x`), so the two multiply.
Drones take the square root of the same curve, because Aether income does not
scale with the blade and a linear multiplier would price the ladder out of reach.
Scrap rerolls and the tech tree stay on their own curves: both are priced by the
resource that pays them.

The server is the only place a price is computed. `ForgeConfig` and
`DroneRegistry` are shared modules with no profile, so they take the multiplier as
an argument and `ProgressionService` supplies it exactly once for the payload and
again for the charge.

### The vault

The backpack key in the side-panel banner opens a full-screen room at 94% x 92%
of the canvas. It is not a tab: four 221x184 cards per row at 1440 design px, and
a permanent 380px inspector on the right holding a live turntable, six stat rows
and an `EQUIP` / `FAVORITE` / `MELT` dock.

Clicking a card inspects it. There is no `STATS` sub-button and no drawer: the
inspector is always on screen, so reading a blade is one click instead of three
gestures. Batch melting is a mode, not a hidden box: `MELT MULTIPLE` arms it, the
key turns into a slate `CANCEL`, every card that owns a meltable copy grows a tick
box, and a click anywhere on a card tray toggles that copy. A red `MELT n` plate
appears at the right end of the bar once something is ticked and settles the batch
through a single `salvageMany` behind a confirm. The four keys sit in fixed slots
left to right — `EQUIP BEST`, `MELT MULTIPLE` / `CANCEL`, `SELECT DUPES`, `CLEAR` —
40px tall with 10px of air, so the strip never reflows as a selection appears.

A card carries a rarity accent bar along the top (`EQUIPPED` when the group holds
the equipped copy, printed inside the same bar), a live `ViewportFrame` render on a
gradient art well, the blade name, a variant tag, the multiplier, an `xN` chip and
a favourite star. The tick box exists only while the mode is armed: an empty
warm-edged box unselected, a gold `✓` when ticked, and no box at all on a group
whose only copies are protected. Thumbnails are built for the rows inside the
scroll window only; a sixty-card inventory keeps about a dozen models mounted.

`EQUIP BEST` sends the server `equipBest`, which compares every copy by
`ForgeConfig.power` and refuses with "Already equipped" rather than paying for a
repaint. `OWNED: 17 / 60` comes from `invMax` on the payload, and the forge refuses
a roll past the ceiling with a reason.

Grouping lives in `InventoryModel` so the grid, the inspector and the batch bar
cannot disagree about what a duplicate is, and so the client's melt count mirrors
the server's rules instead of guessing at them.

### Onboarding

One card, on the first join, and then nothing. The card is the same shape as the
offline `WELCOME BACK` popup: a dimmed scrim, a 480x300 plate, one line of copy and
one CTA (`START FORGING`). Dismissing it writes `guideSeen` and `tutorialCompleted`,
and the controller stops: no second card, no objective strip, no floating tracker,
no fallback card on a slow join.

The top bar's `? GUIDE` replays the card without touching either flag, which is the
only way to see it again after the first session. While the card is live the same
button reads `SKIP GUIDE` and ends it the same way the CTA does.

The old guide was eight cards gated on heat, venting, the first forge, the first
drone, the first rune, ascension and tech, with a 44px objective strip filling the
gaps and staying for the rest of the session. That is eight interruptions plus a
card that never leaves, and it also had a wiring bug that made it worse: `modalCta`
was never assigned, so the CTA only hid the card and the guide advanced itself on
the next step's predicate. The lesson table is gone with it; see
[ADR-047](design-decisions.md).

The ring survives, because the card uses it to point at the anvil while it is open.
It is still an overlay rather than a child of its target: `TutorialHighlightLayer`
is a top-level canvas sibling at `ZIndex 50`, created before the modal so the card
stays above it, and its screen rectangle is copied from the target every
`RenderStepped`. Parenting it inside the target put it at the mercy of
`ClipsDescendants`; the overlay version clamps itself to the visible window.

See [ADR-017](design-decisions.md), [ADR-028](design-decisions.md),
[ADR-036](design-decisions.md) and [ADR-047](design-decisions.md).

### Type scale: nothing interactive sits under 13px

`Theme.Text` is the only type ladder in the client, and the bottom two rungs were
the problem: `Micro = 12` and `Tiny = 10`. The canvas is fit-scaled, so 10 design px
lands at 7 screen px on a 1366-wide laptop — the rank counter, the shard balance and
the rune descriptions were all unreadable at the size they actually rendered.

| Token | Before | After | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Hero` | 50 | 50 | the run meter numeral |
| `Title` | 32 | 32 | modals, the forge header |
| `Heading` | 23 | 23 | panel titles |
| `Subheading` | 19 | 19 | stat readouts, card titles |
| `Body` | 17 | 17 | blade names, row titles |
| `Small` | 15 | 15 | multipliers, hint lines |
| `Micro` | 12 | **14** | captions, cost lines, socket prompts |
| `Tiny` | 10 | **13** | dense rows, badges, lesson counters |

Tab pills are 16px `GothamBlack`. Measured in Studio with `TextService:GetTextSize`:
the widest label (`BLADES`) is 60px at 16, and a tab in the 468px panel is 84.8px,
so the five-tab strip carries the larger type with 24px of air either side.

Card geometry moved with the type, because a 13px line needs a 18–20px band and the
old cards gave it 14–16. The mastery card is 178 tall (was 156), a rune row 68 (was
58), a mission row 48 (was 42), the expedition band 186 (was 168), the heat rail and
its vent key 40 (was 34). Secondary copy switched from warm `textFaint` to a new cool
`textSecondary` (`#B8C2D6`), which holds contrast against the brown card stock
instead of sinking into it.

The store row is the exception: it is **104 tall** with a **72px badge**, not 88 with
48. The row is the only place the panel had to grow for it — `Layout.SIDE_WIDTH` went
432 → 468 and the forge card gave up the same 36. See "Store row geometry" below.

Verified live on all five tabs with `TextLabel.TextFits`: **zero clipped labels**.

### Panel controls have three states, and they are visible

`UiKit.actionButton` is the single CTA control in the side panel. A ready control is
a solid accent gradient with a lit top bevel and white stroked text; a locked one is a
flat `#181C26` slate plate with a dim face and its cost; an owned-but-not-worn blade
shows `✓ EQUIPPED` as a low-alpha green badge. Blade and tech cards carry a 4px
rarity bar down the left edge and a live progress bar toward the price or the next
level. The active tab is a solid accent pill; inactive tabs are flat slate with no
glow, so the selected tab is the only one carrying colour. A store row with no
Marketplace id behind it shows `SOON` instead of a price and answers a click with a
toast, so the storefront never quotes `R$ 149` for something that cannot be bought.

The store does not sell raw currency. Star-Matter and Aether packs would convert
cash into the number being ground, which is the one purchase the analysis says
kills the long game. What is on the shelf is acceleration and utility: Fast Forge,
Auto-Vent, 2x Star-Matter, VIP Aura (status badge), Eternal Forge (+25% forever)
as passes, and Starter Forge Pack (5 Robux: 25 Aether plus fifteen minutes of 2x),
Instant Overdrive, Emergency Vent and Express Salvage as products. The Starter
Pack's timer writes `boostX2Until`; `globalMultiplier` reads that field, which it
did not before, so the 2x now actually pays.

The store row is the one card in the panel that had to grow, and it dragged the panel

| forge card width | 806 | 770 | the 36px the panel took |

The reason the badge grew is the lesson from the first art pass, not taste. The store

The forge card is the one that paid for it. It is a 3D viewport with a full-width
heat rail, and losing 36px costs it nothing; the store column had no width left to
give. Everything downstream reads `Layout`, so the tutorial ring and the objective
strip followed the change without edits.

### Store art

All nine rows now carry a badge: five `GAME PASSES` (the header read `PERMANENT
UPGRADES` before) and four `BOOSTS & UTILITY` products. Products were previously
hardcoded to `icon = nil`; `ProductDef` gained `icon: string?` and the row passes
`def.icon` through, so the same fallback applies — no key, or a key with no id pasted
in, keeps the text pill.

Every id lives in `AssetManifest.GamepassIcons`, grouped into the two tiers. The four
product ids are the same art used for the game-pass badges where the item is a pass,
and are Developer Product icons where it is not.

### Gamepass badges

The STORE tab shows real art for a pass when it exists. `MonetizationConfig` carries
`icon = "fast_forge"` next to the pass, `AssetManifest.gamepassIcon("fast_forge")`
resolves it to an `rbxthumb://` URL, and `ShopView` draws a 72×72 circular well with a
2px `#0F1117` ring and the badge `Fit` inside.

A pass with no art is not broken — it keeps the 56×20 text pill (`FAST`, `AUTO`).
That is the fallback for a fresh clone with zero uploads, and it is why
`gamepassIcon` returns `""` rather than `rbxassetid://0`: the view branches on empty
to pick the pill, and a placeholder id would branch the wrong way into an empty
`ImageLabel`.

Badge ids live in `AssetManifest.GamepassIcons`, a third table beside `Assets` and
`Meshes`, for the reason `Meshes` is separate: `scripts/upload_assets.py` rewrites
only `AssetManifest.Assets`, so a hand-pasted badge id survives regeneration.
Keys match `scripts/generate_gamepass_icons.py`'s `PASSES` list exactly.

**Upload as `Image`, not Decal.** An Open Cloud `assetType: "Decal"` upload stores a
Decal object; `ImageLabel.Image` wants a bitmap. Web-uploaded decals do work in an
`ImageLabel` (their id really is the image), which is what makes this easy to get
wrong.

**Read badges through the thumbnail CDN, not `rbxassetid://`.** The raw asset route
does not serve these Open Cloud uploads here — `CreateEditableImageAsync` returns
`HttpError` for the badge Image, the badge Decal and an earlier badge Image alike —
while `rbxthumb://type=Asset&id=<id>&w=420&h=420` returns a real 420x420 RGBA bitmap
with transparency intact. `AssetManifest.gamepassIcon` builds that URL from the
stored id, so the table stays readable and callers stay unchanged. 420 is a served
size; 512 is rejected, and 420 is ~12x the 34.5px the store actually draws.

**An image is on screen when `IsLoaded` is `true`.** Not when the frame has the right
size. `ImageLabel.IsLoaded` was read as unreliable for most of this investigation
because it was `false` everywhere — including for `rbxasset://` built-ins used as a
control. It was reporting the truth. `ContentProvider:PreloadAsync` *is* useless as a
gate here (it returns `Failure` for every id, built-ins included), but `IsLoaded` is
not. A failing remote image also logs `ContentId: ... failed due to: <reason>`, and
`Authentication required to access Asset` (not distributed / raw route refusing),
`Asset type does not match requested type` (wrong id type) and silence (loaded) mean
three different things.

**The badge is drawn at 51.8 real px, and the field still has to be bright.** The
row is 104 design px, the well is 72, and the canvas runs at 0.719 scale. The first
art composition was honest work — a steel hammer on an obsidian disc — and at the
old 34.5 real px it measured p50 = 28 luminance with 7% of pixels above 120, which
on a store row is a black circle. The shipped composition inverts it: gold field
(`FIELD_HOT` to `FIELD_DEEP`), ink subject, p50 = 112 and 43% of pixels above 120.
If you add a badge, shrink it to 48px and 35px before uploading; if it stops
reading, the field is too dark.

Add the next badge with:

```
python scripts/generate_gamepass_icons.py        # writes assets/generated/gamepasses/*.png
# upload the PNG with assetType "Image", paste the id into AssetManifest.GamepassIcons
```

`--only <pass>` renders one badge and exits non-zero naming any pass with no
renderer written, so a half-finished set cannot ship a blank.

### The Salvage loop (RNG, timers, codex)

The retention pass from `incremental/video-06-mvp-rng-session-time.md` ships as the
Salvage rack in the Fleet tab. Three levers, one band:

1. **The roll happens at drop.** Every 40 accepted strikes fills the meter. When it
   fills, `SalvageConfig.rollRarity()` picks Scrap / Alloy / Prime / Void at
   58 / 28 / 10 / 4, and the sealed slot is drawn in that rarity's colour. The
   player can want a specific cache before opening it.
2. **Cracking runs on the clock.** `crackCache` sets `readyAt = os.time() +
   crackSeconds` (0 / 45 / 180 / 600). A Prime cache keeps the session open; a Void
   cache started before bed is ready on rejoin.
3. **The codex is the long goal.** Twelve sigils across four sets of three. A
   completed set is a permanent multiplier (+6% strike, +8% cooling, +3% crit,
   +12% vent), returned in the same shape as a rune bonus.

The rack holds three. A full rack stops the meter, so hoarding is a visible choice
rather than a silent loss. Rewards are computed at claim, scaled by the equipped
blade and the global multiplier.

Server authority: `crackCache` and `claimCache` validate the slot, the rarity and
the clock. The payload carries `cacheMeter`, `cacheGoal`, at most three
`{ rarity, readyAt, secondsLeft }` entries, `sigils` and `codexSets`.

The rack is typographic on purpose: it ships with no art and still reads. Optional
art prompts for the four caches and four set emblems are in
`user-manual/16-salvage-art-prompts.txt`.

### List badges (Fleet, Runes, Tech)

Fifteen badges, one per list item: three expeditions, four runes and eight tech
nodes. They live in **`assets/badges/`**, which is permanent art, not generated
output: `assets/badges/*.png` are the shipped 512px discs, and
`assets/badges/source/` holds the original JPGs plus a contact sheet. Nothing in
that folder is written by a script.

They arrived from Nano Banana 2 and were normalised with the same tool as the store
badges:

```
python scripts/prepare_gamepass_art.py --src assets/badges/source/rune_ember.jpg \
  --dst assets/badges/rune_ember.png --no-preview
```

The tool keys the border-connected bright background, clamps to the disc, rescales
to the 0.94 diameter ratio, and writes RGBA with transparent corners. All fifteen
measure `corners (0,0,0,0)` and an alpha bbox around `(13..28, 13..28, 998, 998)`.

Ids live in **`AssetManifest.UiArt`**, a fourth table beside `Assets`, `Meshes` and
`GamepassIcons`, and resolve through `AssetManifest.uiArt(key)` to the same
`rbxthumb://` CDN route the store badges use. A missing key returns `""`, and
`UiKit.badgeWell` returns nil for `""`, so a clone with no uploads keeps text-only
rows instead of drawing an empty grey circle.

`UiKit.badgeWell` is the one shared well: a circular plate, a `Crop`-scaled
thumbnail and a 2px `#0F1117` ring, with an optional 1px accent stroke. It is used
at three sizes.

| list | art | row | change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fleet expeditions | **64px** | 72 (was 54) | band 204 -> 266, shelf 170 -> 228, pitch 56 -> 76 |
| Rune shelf | **88px** | 104 (was 78) | text starts at x 108, column 194 wide |
| Tech tree | **80px** | 132 (was 106) | name and description start at x 104 |

The second pass doubled the art because the first pass was still reading as a
decorated bullet point. Fleet and runes scroll, so height is paid for by scrolling,
not by cutting copy. The tech card is the only one that needed row height: an 80px
badge beside a 24px title and a two-line description does not fit in 106px.

Measured live at 1280x720 design px on the 0.719 canvas, before the canvas widened
to 1440 and the side panel to 520. The rows still hold; a wider panel only adds
slack at the right edge.

```
Mission_sweep  row 412x72   art 64 loaded=true  labels fit 2/2
Rune_ember     row 432x104  art 88 loaded=true  labels fit 3/3
Tech card      row 432x132  art 80 loaded=true  labels fit 4/4  (x8)
```


### Supplied art (the AI-drawn badges)

`auto_vent` did not come out of the procedural generator. It was drawn by an image
model and dropped in as a JPEG, which means two things the generator never has to
deal with: RGB only, and the "transparent" checkerboard painted as actual pixels. A
JPEG has no alpha channel, so that checkerboard would have shipped as a grey flag
square around the disc.

`scripts/prepare_gamepass_art.py` handles it and is the entry point for every
AI-drawn badge from here:

```
python scripts/prepare_gamepass_art.py --src assets/generated/gamepasses/Auto-vent.jpg
```

It keys out only the **border-connected** low-saturation bright pixels, so the warm
white steam inside the disc survives, then clamps a circle so a lone checkerboard
speck cannot drag the crop square, then rescales the disc to the same 0.94 diameter
ratio the procedural badges use. Deterministic: same input bytes, same output bytes.
It prints the luminance numbers and writes a `_<name>_at_size.png` strip at 64/48/35
so the thumbnail read is checked rather than assumed.

`ImageDraw.floodfill` is **not** used, and that is not a style preference. It still
exists in Pillow 13 but returns without filling anything — a silent no-op on a mask
where a BFS finds 388,118 pixels. A keying step that quietly does nothing is worse
than one that crashes, because the output still looks like an image. `SUPPLIED` in
`generate_gamepass_icons.py` records which passes came from outside, so
`--only auto_vent` says "supplied art" and exits 0 instead of claiming a renderer is
missing.

`--ring-clamp` exists for generators that draw the disc on a patterned plate instead
of a plain checkerboard. `x2_star_matter` came back as a disc sitting on a dotted
grey plate inside a white margin: the background key removed only the white band, and
the plate would have shown as a square behind the circle. The flag cuts at the disc's
own ink contour — the outermost radius whose whole circumference is dark — and falls
back to the percentile clamp when no ring clears the threshold.

### Creating the Marketplace items

The art is one problem; the listings are another, and the two are separate APIs.

Roblox shipped Open Cloud endpoints for both in December 2025, still BETA:

| what | endpoint | scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Game Pass | `POST /game-passes/v1/universes/{universeId}/game-passes` | `game-pass:write` |
| Developer Product | `POST /developer-products/v2/universes/{universeId}/developer-products` | `developer-product:write` |

Both take `multipart/form-data` and an **`imageFile` part** — a file upload, not an
asset id. That is the trap: a JSON body with `imageId` is rejected, and the error is a
bare `Scope not authorized` if the key also lacks the scope, so the two failures look
identical from the outside.

`scripts/create_store_items.py` does all nine in one pass, prints the id map, and with
`--apply` writes the ids back into `MonetizationConfig.luau`:

```
python scripts/create_store_items.py --check   # what exists, what would be created
python scripts/create_store_items.py           # create, print ids
python scripts/create_store_items.py --apply   # create and patch the config
```

The universe id (`10775534186`) is resolved once from the place id and cached in the
script; re-resolve with `GET /universes/v1/places/{placeId}/universe` if the place
moves. `--check` needs `game-pass:read` to list; without it, it reports zero existing
rather than failing, which is why the real run also handles a duplicate gracefully by
printing the id rather than erroring.

Every surface is opaque and carries a 1px rule; headers are flat steel plates,
modals are a flat plate on a dim scrim, and the only heavy outline is the strike key
(ADR-076). The forge has no deck: the 240×64 strike key, 380×78 heat dial and 136×64
purge key float over a full-bleed stage, so nothing covers the blade (ADR-078). The
left rail owns navigation; the top bar is chrome plus the utility keys and the two
verbs. Layout numbers for the rail, bar and content rect live in one
`src/client/Layout.luau`, required by `Main` and `HudController`, because duplicated
geometry is how a row drifts into its neighbour's gutter.

### Feedback and telemetry

`NOTES` sits beside `GUIDE` in the top-bar utility row and opens a modal with four
categories (`BUG`, `BALANCE`, `CONFUSING`, `OTHER`), a 240 character box and a send
key. The server trims the text, refuses anything under four characters, and writes a
record to `QuantumForge_Feedback`; a failed write is warned and printed rather than
thrown.

`Funnel` logs one AnalyticsService event per milestone per session on the server:
`Session_Start`, `First_Strike`, `First_SweetSpot`, `First_Vent`,
`Forge_<bladeId>`, `First_Drone`, `First_Expedition`, `First_Rune`, `First_Socket`,
`First_Overdrive`, `First_Ascension`, `Tutorial_Done`, `Feedback_Sent`. The call
was originally made from the client and threw `AnalyticsService can only be
executed on the game server`; it lives on the server now, inside a `pcall`.

### VFX sprite sheets

The effects are generated, not drawn by hand. `generate_vfx.py` builds five
effects — `slash`, `impact`, `vent`, `overheat`, `aura` — as four 4×4 flipbooks
each (`glow`, `rim`, `body`, `hotcore`) at 512px per frame, plus two single-image
particles. Every sheet is pure white with an alpha channel, so the runtime tints
one upload to any blade rarity; nothing is re-exported per tier.

```powershell
python scripts/generate_vfx.py                  # 22 sheets + manifest + review board
python scripts/generate_vfx.py --review         # all 16 frames per effect, 192px cells
python scripts/generate_vfx.py --layers impact  # one effect, one row per layer
python scripts/generate_vfx.py --only slash impact
python scripts/generate_vfx.py --frame 256 --no-manifest
python scripts/upload_assets.py --vfx --dry-run
python scripts/upload_assets.py --vfx --creator user:123456789
```

The generator prints the measured edge margin per effect and exits non-zero if
any frame violates the 4px rule — a sheet that would show its own bounding box
cannot ship. Output is deterministic: two runs produce byte-identical PNGs.

Sheets are white-on-alpha, so opening one directly looks empty. Composite them
on a dark background before judging them (`assets/generated/vfx/review.png` is
exactly that).

If the IDs are still `rbxassetid://0`, `SpriteFx.play` returns `false` and every
call site falls back to `Vfx.burst`. `aura` is the one effect with no procedural
equivalent: without its sheet the anvil simply has no halo.

### Blade meshes (Blender → Open Cloud)

`scripts/build_models.py` sculpts every blade in the catalogue, plus the fleet
drone, without anyone modelling by hand. It parses `BladeRegistry.luau` exactly
the way the icon generator does, so adding a blade to the Luau table is enough to
get it into the build.

```powershell
# Blender is not on PATH after a default Windows install, and its version folder
# changes on every upgrade — the launcher resolves it for you.
scripts\blender.cmd build_models.py     # -> assets/models/*.fbx + index.json
scripts\blender.cmd render_models.py    # -> assets/models/contact_sheet.png

# outside Blender
python scripts/upload_assets.py --mesh --dry-run
python scripts\upload_assets.py --mesh --creator user:123456789
```

The uploader reads the Open Cloud key from `ROBLOX_OPEN_CLOUD_KEY` or
`ROBLOX_API_KEY` — either name works, so an existing `setx ROBLOX_API_KEY` keeps
functioning. It is never passed on the command line.

`scripts\blender.cmd` forwards to `--background --python`, so it needs no GUI.
Set `BLENDER_EXE` if your install is somewhere unusual; otherwise it checks
PATH, then `%ProgramFiles%\Blender Foundation\Blender*\` newest-first. The same
script runs inside a live Blender through the DCC-MCP gateway with
`blender_scripting__execute_script_file`.

What the builder guarantees, because the engine cares about every line of it:

| Property | Value | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Triangles | 586–1 064 per blade, 21 540 total | Budget is 2 500 each; two lanes of 16 rings plus a 6-part guard |
| Height | 4.0 / 4.6 / 5.2 / 5.8 / 6.4 studs by tier, +0.0 to +0.28 from grip and guard proportions | Sized in the asset, not per-model in Studio |
| Orientation | tip +Y, cutting edge −Z, thickness X | FBX `axis_forward="−Z"`, `axis_up="Y"` |
| Origin | base of the hilt, exactly (0, 0, 0) | A pivot at the bounding-box centre mounts a sword half underground |
| Objects | two per asset: `<id>` and `<id>_glow` | One material per MeshPart: metal body, `Neon` core. `rivet_edge` has no emissive geometry, so it exports one |
| Shading | flat, no textures | Colour and `Material` come from the registry at runtime |

**Two objects, not one.** Roblox gives a MeshPart a single `Material`, so the
only way to have a glowing core inside a metal blade is to ship them as separate
objects. `InsertService:LoadAsset` returns the parts with their names intact —
verified live, `comets_wake` and `comets_wake_glow` both arrive — and
`BladeModelBuilder` classifies by name, giving the `_glow` half `Neon` and the
body an opaque metal.

**The body is never `Neon` or `Glass`, whatever the registry says.** Those
values described the primitive build (an emissive slab, or a transparent shell
with light inside it). On a sculpted mesh an emissive body flattens the shading
that makes the bevels legible, and a glass body is literally see-through —
measured live, Comet's Wake rendered as a transparent shell around its core,
which reads as a hollow sword. The energy lives in `_glow`; the body is metal.

`render_models.py` re-imports every FBX and renders `assets/models/contact_sheet.png`,
printing measured bounds and pivot offset per file. That is the check that the
export survived the axis conversion.

Uploads share the icons' resumable cache keyed on `geometryHash`, so a rebuild
only re-uploads the models whose geometry actually changed. `--mesh` writes
`AssetManifest.Meshes`, a *separate* table from `AssetManifest.Assets`, because a
mesh id and an image id are both `rbxassetid://N` and nothing in the type system
stops you passing one to an `ImageLabel`.

**Rendered in-game.** `MeshService` (`src/server/MeshService.luau`) reads the
manifest at boot and pulls every uploaded Model through
`InsertService:LoadAsset`, four at a time, into `ReplicatedStorage.BladeModels`
keyed by asset id — blades and the drone alike. Measured on a live server:
**26/26 in ~9s**; serial was ~35s.

`BladeModelBuilder` then clones the Model into its `ViewportFrame`, measuring and
rescaling it — the FBX imports at 100× (a 4-stud blade arrives 400 studs tall),
so `entry.height` from `index.json` is the target, not the imported size. Until a
mesh lands the blade draws from its procedural recipe, so the game is fully
playable with zero uploads; `BladeModelBuilder.watch` fires when the asset
replicates and rebuilds in place.

**Both 3D stages spin and orbit independently.** `ViewportOrbitController` owns
two motions that never consult each other: the model turns on Y at 0.9 rad/s every
frame, and the camera orbits the framed centre at the player's yaw and pitch.

Drag with **left or right mouse button** to move the *camera*: LMB anywhere on the
3D view, RMB anywhere — the habit every other 3D viewer on the platform trains.
Touch drags too. Once a drag starts it keeps orbiting even if the cursor leaves the
frame, pitch clamps at ±40°, and releasing leaves the camera where the player put
it. Three stages share it: the forge anvil, the fleet hero and the vault inspector.
The press has to come from a control that sits over the render — the forge's
`StrikeButton` and the vault's `VaultStageGrab` — because a `ViewportFrame` never
receives a mouse press and a modal's scrim eats it first. A press that starts a
drag on the anvil still strikes once: the strike fires on press, and the
alternative would put a full click of latency on the core loop.

The wash behind every stage is composited, not painted: one opaque tier ramp plus
four crossed gradient bands, each transparent at both ends. Where they overlap the
colours add up under the blade and the corners stay dark — the same fog a radial
would give, with none of the stepping a stack of circles leaves. The forge and the
fleet pass `Theme.tierBackdrop`, the vault passes the blade's rarity and variant,
and `StageBackdrop.setColors` re-tints in place on an equip or a card click.

Two measured traps in that control, both of which made dragging do nothing:

| Trap | What happens | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ViewportFrame.Active = true` | Every click on the frame is marked **game-processed**, so `UserInputService.InputBegan` never sees it — and the frame does not fire `InputBegan` either. Measured: `processed=true` with no frame event | `Active = false`; input is resolved globally against the frame rectangle |
| `GetMouseLocation()` vs `AbsolutePosition` | The mouse call reports from the top of the screen while a non-inset `ScreenGui` starts below the top bar. Measured: a click at `445,200` read back as `GetMouseLocation() = 445,258` | Compare against `InputObject.Position`, which is in the same space as `AbsolutePosition` |
| `InputObject.Delta` on a warped cursor | A synthetic or mouselocked move reports a position with a zero delta, so the drag applies nothing | Deltas are computed from successive `Position` values |

The white impact wash is full-bleed over the stage (`UDim2.fromScale(1, 1)`): the
old `1, -22` stopped 22px short and left a lit strip along the bottom edge.

Three engine traps this cost, all worth knowing before touching the viewport:

| Trap | What happens | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Model:GetBoundingBox()` and scaled MeshParts | A MeshPart keeps its native `MeshSize` (640) while `Size` carries the scaled value (6.4). With any other part in the model, GetBoundingBox reports the **unscaled** extents — a 6.4-stud blade measured 320.7 | Compute the AABB from `CFrame` and `Size` (`BladeModelBuilder.bounds`) |
| `Model:PivotTo` with no `PrimaryPart` | The pivot is whatever the engine last computed, so `PivotTo` translates the model by an extra 0.22 studs on the first frame after framing | An invisible `Pivot` part at the hilt base, set as `PrimaryPart` |
| A one-shot asset callback | Measured live: 6 of 25 thumbnails upgraded, the rest kept the placeholder with no second chance | The thumbnail cache key includes "is the mesh here", so the 10 Hz refresh self-heals in one redraw |

**The split blades are cut across their width, and decorations are sized against the
blade's thickness.** Two geometry bugs, one report each. The first split build offset two
full-width blades along X and left a slot between them, so the seam was visible straight
through the sword and it read as hollow. `loft(gap=…)` now emits an `edge` lane and a
`spine` lane sharing the outer silhouette, with the cut closing over the last fifth so
the tip is one point. The second was the rune and vent plates: `width × 0.30` across the
face put a 0.655-stud bar through a 0.135-stud blade, which is what actually looked like
a hole in the steel. Marks on the flat are `thickness × 1.2` in X and `width × 0.42` in
Y; only the span across the face uses the width.
