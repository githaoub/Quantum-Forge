# The vault index

Every design note in the repository lives here; the repo `README.md` is the only
`.md` outside the vault. One naming rule: **the filename says what the note is for.**
A video analysis is `video-NN-<subject>.md`, where `NN` is the order it was watched
and `<subject>` is the three or four words that describe the lever it teaches. No
more `VIDEOANALYSIS-04.md` with no clue whether it is about VFX, pricing, or swords.

## Naming rules

| prefix | what it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `video-NN-<subject>.md` | one video, broken down, then mapped onto this game |
| `build-log-<change>.md` | what actually shipped for a given analysis, measured |
| `art-prompts-<tool>.md` | how to write prompts for a given generator |
| `code-map.md` | module-by-module map of `src/` and where to change things |
| `project-readme.md` | the long-form project record through Forge 12; UI sections superseded by the ADRs |
| `game-design-brief.md` | the founding design document, kept current |
| `game-design-math.md` | every formula and table behind the economy |
| `game-design-research.md` | text-first research: loops, retention, fairness, UI, with an audit |
| `design-decisions.md` | every ADR, newest last |

Every video note ends with an **Application to Quantum Forge** block or a
**Summary Action Checklist**. That block is the reason the note exists. If a note
ever stops having one, it is source material, not a plan. A research note ends
with an audit and a ranked action list, and says what was actually read.

## The one-line versions

**06:** Speed to market, RNG before the reward, a real timer on the good stuff,
and a collection that cannot be finished in one sitting. The shipped answer is the
Salvage rack in `ADR-042`: caches drop on a strike meter with their rarity already
rolled, cracking runs on the unix clock, and twelve sigils across four sets pay
permanent multipliers.

**07:** The blade ladder became a forge. Five banners roll by run
Star-Matter, rarity and variant roll on separate weights, every copy is kept, and
duplicates pay the Scrap that rerolls a variant. The button count, sound kit and
screen effects grew to match; the settings sheet persists all three preferences.
The inventory left the sidebar at the same time: a `VAULT` key opens a full-screen
grid that groups identical copies, melts a selection in one request, and slides a
detail drawer over the screen. Rolling got an object between the press and the
reward: a crate slams, winds up, holds and shatters before the blade appears.

**08:** The fleet stopped being one drone with a ceiling. Five rungs (Scrap, Molten,
Quantum, Celestial, Cosmic) with no cap and rising efficiency, gated on fleet size
rather than currency, each drawn apart in the stage. The forge stopped having a
black box under the blade: heat rail, streak pips, yield and the strike prompt now
sit on a scrim inside a 486px stage that is itself the strike target. Helper copy
came out, and the two text keys in the banner became a backpack and a gear drawn in
`UiKit` primitives.

**09:** The vault became a room instead of a drawer: four 221px cards, a permanent
380px inspector, a canvas widened to 1440x720 so both fit, favourites as a
per-uid server-enforced set, and a split view whose card click inspects. The forge
roll price climbed `1.05x` per roll to a `12x` ceiling and reset per banner, and
onboarding collapsed to one first-join card latched by `guideSeen`.

**10:** Every Star-Matter sink took an account multiplier anchored on lifetime
Star-Matter (`sqrt(lifetime / 1e9)`, capped at `1e4`), because drones multiply the
passive yield by the blade and a fixed table stops being a sink. The batch bar
learned to speak copies: a tick exists only where a copy will melt, a protected
copy turns `x3` into `2/3`, and `MELT MULTIPLE` sits next to `EQUIP BEST` in the
house red at 46px. The settings sheet's rows became the switches, and the gear's
teeth were rotated ninety degrees so they point out of the rim instead of across
it.

**11:** Melting became a mode. `MELT MULTIPLE` arms it, turns into a slate
`CANCEL`, shows a tick box only where a copy can melt, and makes the whole card
tray the hit target; a red `MELT n` plate appears at the right end of the bar and
is the only thing that deletes. The strip is four fixed 40px slots with 10px of
air. Every stage backdrop is now a composited wash - one opaque ramp and four
crossed gradient bands, each transparent at both ends - instead of a stack of
circles, and the forge and the inspector both orbit on a held drag, pressed from
a control over the render rather than from the viewport.

**12:** The crate stopped being a button and became an object: a 3D crate that
falls, winds up and shatters, priced against the account's income instead of a
fixed table. The roll grew two modifier axes, the codex became a 1120px ten-page
manual, leaderboards and the trade desk shipped, and the blade catalogue got a
quality ladder and custom currency art. It is the largest single pass in the
record.

**13:** The UI stopped being seven screens built from seven vocabularies. Foundry
Instrument gave it one palette, one type ladder, one accent, a left rail and a
single repainted section banner; the forge lost its deck and its roll column; heat
got a lean point at 90% instead of a lockout; and CRATES, FLEET, RUNES and STORE
were rebuilt around one focal object each.

**14:** The crate reveal became a ceremony: drop, six-beat rattle, held charge,
crack, then the copy, with rays and a beam above rank 3. A ten-pull shows every
copy it paid for, pity reads as progress, `BACK TO POOL` exits a result, and two
visible rules now match what the server pays (the band clamps at 90, the gauge
repaints from the payload).

**15:** The ceremony left the card and took the screen. The scrim is sized to the
viewport plus the GUI inset, the wash is full-bleed, the store and fleet canvases
are measured in canvas units, and the store leads with VIP and Starter Forge.

**16:** The result got an afterglow. A batch lists every copy on a rail, the
`COLLECT` key is locked for half a second and then closes behind a claim flash,
the sealed beat holds for 0.62 s with a beam, a vignette, a riser and two
heartbeats, and clicking inside a panel no longer closes the vault. The store's
nine listings are live, with VIP above Eternal Forge and below the sum.
